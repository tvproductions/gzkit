## DoD evolutionary acquisition (secondary)

Source: CRS Report RS21195 (2004), p. 1. Secondary.

> DOD's goals in using EA/SD are to :
> • get useful increments of new capability into the hands of U.S. personnel more quickly ;
> • take better advantage of user feedback in refining system requirements and developing subsequent increments of capability ;

Source: CRS Report RS21195 (2004), p. 2. Secondary.

> The core idea of EA/SD is to set aside the quest for 100% fulfillment of the requirement in the initial version of the weapon and instead rapidly develop an initial version that meets some fraction (for example, 50% to 60%) of the requirement . Field experience with this initial version is then be used to develop later versions, or blocks, of the weapon that meet an increasing fraction of the requirement, until a version is eventually developed that meets the 100% standard .

> Each phase is governed by certain acquisition rules and regulations, including entrance and exit criteria, and is subject to the requirements process, including the Initial Capabilities Document (ICD) and Capability Development Document (CDD) . Each block includes its own acquisition contracts and fully funded budgets for a defined time period .

(Spacing before punctuation is as extracted from the scanned PDF.)

## Shape as stated

As the CRS report describes it, DoD evolutionary acquisition delivers **increments** of capability in successive **blocks** (versions). Each block meets a growing fraction of the overall requirement and runs its own phases, entrance/exit criteria, requirements documents (ICD, CDD), contracts and fully funded budget. The primary DoD instructions and the TOGAF capability-based planning text were not available, so their own containers are not established here.
