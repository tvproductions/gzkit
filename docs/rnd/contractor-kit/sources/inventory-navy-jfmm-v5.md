## 5. Departure From Specification (Part I, Chapter 8)

### 5.1 Definitions and purpose

| Item | Verbatim | Para |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | "To establish standard procedures to be used by the Fleet and all maintenance activities for reporting, requesting approval and clearance, at the earliest opportunity, of all non-conforming or departable conditions, Departures from Specification (DFS)." | I-8.1 |
| Specification | "Specifications are engineered requirements such as type of materials, dimensional clearances, vibration levels, flow rates, and physical arrangement to which ship components are purchased, installed, tested, and maintained. … There are occasions when the applicable specifications cannot be met. In these cases, the nonconformance to specifications is controlled with a DFS." | I-8.1.1 |
| DFS (mechanism) | "A DFS is the mechanism used to document and resolve a lack of compliance with any authoritative document, plan, procedure, instruction, etc., except in instances where NAVSEA has signed and issued formal correspondence adjudicating one of the preceding such as a Liaison Action Request (LAR), Engineering Request (ER), or similar documents." | I-8.1.2.a |
| DFS (short form) | "A DFS (non-nuclear only) is a lack of compliance with any authoritative document, plan, procedure, instruction, etc." | I-8.2 |
| Authorized Technical Authority | "An Authorized Technical Authority is a representative designated by reference (a) to act for Naval Sea Systems Command (NAVSEA) in dispositioning nonconformance issues. They are also charged with providing technically sound maintenance alternatives to the Fleet upon which maintenance and operational decisions can be made." | I-8.1.2.b |
| Deviation vs waiver | "The terms deviation and waiver are often used synonymously. The principal difference being that deviations are requested prior to conducting work that will result in a non-conformance and waivers are requested after a nonconformance has been discovered." | I-8.1.2.d |
| Minor items that skip the DFS | "As determined by the ship and ISIC, temporary nonconformances, except SUBSAFE, Scope of Certification, and Fly-by-Wire nonconformances, that are minor which would not benefit from an engineering analysis or risk assessment will be entered into the CSMP. CSMP entries of this type do not require a corresponding DFS to adjudicate the condition and will be scheduled for repair in a future maintenance availability." | I-8.1.2.c |
| Order of precedence | "WHERE THIS MANUAL IS MORE RESTRICTIVE THAN OTHERS, THIS MANUAL WILL TAKE PRECEDENCE UNLESS SPECIFICALLY STATED THAT IT PROVIDES GUIDANCE ONLY. UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS ON PRECEDENCE SHOULD BE REFERRED TO THE TYCOM FOR RESOLUTION. IN SOME INSTANCES A SPECIFICATION OF LESSER PRECEDENCE MAY MODIFY OR SUPERSEDE A MORE SENIOR SPECIFICATION" | I-8.1.3 NOTE |
| Nuclear: no DFS | "Request for DFS for nuclear systems will be neither requested nor approved. If a ship or FMA has a question, problem, or is unable to comply with nuclear specifications, request for technical resolution will be made using a LAR. Formal resolution of the LAR is required prior to reactor plant or propulsion plant startup." | I-8.4 |

[reading] Chapter 8 separates two axes that a software regime often merges: severity (Major / Minor, set by what the nonconformance affects) and duration (Temporary / Permanent, set by whether the condition will be repaired). Approval authority is a function of both: permanent acceptance of a nonconformance goes to the design authority (NAVSEA) unless an identical precedent exists; temporary acceptance stays with the operating chain (TYCOM / ISIC, or CO at sea). Scope change, by contrast, is handled by FWP/CWP revision (Ch. 2), not by DFS — a DFS accepts a non-conforming *result*; a revision changes the *work*. The only explicit crossover is the test requirement: a DFS can stand in for a revision when the test changes (I-2.3.7.3.a(3)).
