## Scope

RO-12 lands the governed apparatus for maintenance work: the positions, kept apart by mechanism; the records of what was isolated, done and left outstanding; the inspection independent of the performer; the runtime that drives each act; and the closure a reviewer witnesses. It keys on RO-5's work-package id and extends the runtime RO-14's tranche 1 moved to the work-package surface, as `kind: repair` (RO-14 ruling 4: one runtime, `kind` selecting the procedure).

The test for the design inside the drydock is the maintenance discipline, not canon's ceremony. Safe mode suspends canon and does not suspend that discipline (record decisions *the design review is judged from the drydock's side* and *the maintenance discipline is the drydock's own*).

It does not land the two-way mode contract across existing controls, which is RO-13, or `gz obpi`'s removal, which is RO-14's tranche 3. The pipeline is carried forward unchanged: lessons cross each way as GHIs.

## Carried from the record, not re-ruled

- **Positions.** The operator, always the commander (A1). The coordinator, the operator-facing session, which performs no work and holds the Authorizing Officer duty (A3). One craftsman persona in two positions, repair and restoration (A4, A6). The Quality Assurance Inspector, dispatched by the runtime and never by the coordinator, its findings reaching the operator unedited (A5). The surveillant, dispatched by the operator only (A8).
- **Records.** The tag-out log of line items, Danger and Caution, each line carrying its removal procedure, written when hung (B6). The maintenance record (B7). The list of discrepancies, graded *blocks return to service* or *due by a named event* (B8). The work package log, a Layer 3 view (B11).
- **Acts.** Initiation, the operator's, then commencement of work, the coordinator's, once every surface has its tag hung (C1). Three tiers of check, (V), second person, (I), a lower tier never satisfying a higher mark (C3). Stop work, a recorded event with a trigger from a closed list, routed to its resolver (C4). Rework, then a buy-back limited to the finding (C5). Final inspection and acceptance, then closed (C10). Shift turnover (C11). Transfer of custody (C12).
- **Rules on the work.** One tag-out per surface, overlapping packages in sequence, a second craftsman only on disjoint surfaces. Every line item legible to a worker not involved in the disconnection (JFMM I-13.2.c). A piece found out of its expected position stops restoration (TUM 1.8.5.b(2)). Each position carries its own obligations, and the vocabulary lives once, in the crosswalk.
- **Model routing.** The pipeline's maps, performers by task-card complexity and the inspector by `REVIEW_MODEL_MAP`, kept current by `frontier-model-card-currency`.

## The cwd-escape guard (#1296)

The issue's acceptance cases, unchanged by ruling, are requirements of this order:

1. A dispatch meant for a throwaway project whose shell is left at the repo root, running a ledger- or canon-writing `gz` verb, is refused before any write, and the ledger line count is unchanged.
2. The same verb run where the dispatch intended proceeds (the valid control).
3. A main-session `gz` command run by the operator in the live checkout is unaffected (second control).

Each is shown by a test that fails if the guard is removed, and one of the three against a real dispatched subagent. The harness facts are in the contract's § Rulings of 2026-11-02. #1296 closes as superseded by this section, which is the requirement's one home (operator, 2026-11-03, verbatim: *"A"*); the guard itself is not yet built.

## Rulings

1. **A position is told by its dispatch record** (operator, by selection: *"Dispatch record (Recommended)"*). The runtime mints a dispatch id on `work_package_dispatched`, bound to the position, the subject and the intended root, and puts it in the prompt it composes. A position-bound verb requires the id and refuses by what the ledger says that dispatch may do. Hooks record `agent_id` beside it where the harness gives one. The limit stands in the open: the id can be copied, so it stops mistakes and shortcuts and not forgery, as with a typed name. Put to the operator: A, this; B, harness identity in hooks, which leaves Codex sessions without the refusals; C, a typed position.
2. **Two pipelines on one runtime** (operator, verbatim: *"yes, two pipelines on one runtime"*, 2026-11-03). Construction and repair are two pipelines, one per column, on the one runtime RO-14's ruling 4 settled. The repair pipeline, on `kind: repair`, keyed on the repair order: entry from a problem report under the operator's initiation of the repair package; the freeze once exploration finds the order's repair requirement, the package open meanwhile; commencement once every surface has its tag hung; the repair, with stop work and the hold on the closed trigger list and the care rule with the governors off; restoration; (V), then (I); rework and buy-back limited to the finding; Gate 5 at the order. The package closes at return to service, the operator's separate act. Its refusals are the witness floor's, keyed to the order. Shared with construction is the runtime only: dispatch, the bound, block and unblock, acceptance and locks (ruling 4), with the order record and Gate 5 at the order; the boundary between the two pipelines is tested. The regime is the mode's (`.gzkit/mx.json`), never the column's. Construction's pipeline is RP-14's to migrate. Put to the operator first as A, the order driven with `kind` selecting the procedure; B, the package driven, orders recorded only for Gate 5; C, a runtime per column, which re-put RO-14's declined option; the operator asked whether the columns are two pipelines, and canon's ruling 4 already said so.
3. **The holder is the MX session** (operator, verbatim: *"mx session makes sense"*, 2026-11-03). A hung tag and the `mx-session` lease are held by the MX session id that `gz mx enter` mints and `gz mx exit` ends, never by the harness session, which each `/clear` replaces. A relief inside one MX session takes over nothing by name; a holder changes only with the MX session. This amends the record's C11 for this order. To land in RP-12: the tag-out log's holder field carries the MX session id, and `gz mx renew` stops writing the harness id as the lease's agent. Raised by the operator: *"i clear context frequently so how will attaching leases to sessions make sense?"*; the alternatives put were the harness session id, the operator `g0` and the agent name. Open point 3 stands for the log's store, its other fields, hanging, clearing and the not-available mark.
4. **The tag-out log is ledger events, the log a derived view** (operator, verbatim: *"A"*, 2026-11-03). Hanging, clearing, a stop request and the not-available mark are each a `gz` verb appending one ledger event; the open log is rendered from them as a Layer 3 view, as the work package log is, and is never edited. An isolation decides what the runtime refuses, so it is Layer 2 fact, written only through `gz` verbs. Put to the operator: A, this; B, a dedicated store file beside the locks, mutable and hand-editable, the shape behind the two lock-file discrepancies due with this log; C, hand-kept lines in the contract, which leave no runtime refusal to witness.
5. **A typed line item the runtime can check** (operator, verbatim: *"A"*, 2026-11-03). The hanging event carries the tag id; Danger or Caution; the **surface** as a typed reference, a registered control (validator, guard or hook, by name) or a repo path; the **position as hung** (off, advisory or recording); the hazard; the removal procedure, legible to a worker not involved (JFMM I-13.2.c); the repair order id and the MX session (ruling 3); the hanging dispatch id (ruling 1) and the coordinator's issue mark; the **source** (the order's own work, a standard disconnect list entry, an MEL (M) bypass, or a surveillant's stop); and the **check level** it was hung under. The clearing event carries the tag id, the clearing dispatch id, the coordinator's authorization and the **position found**, expected or out of position, the latter stopping restoration (TUM 1.8.5.b(2)). The typed surface lets refusal 2 and one tag-out per surface be computed; the recorded positions make the restorer's stop mechanical. Put to the operator: A, this; B, a minimal item with a free-text surface, which leaves refusal 2 and the stop to judgment; C, A plus a compatibility check against the check level at hanging, as the TUM's Tag Guide List against plant condition, waiting on RP-13's standby levels and addable later since A records the level.
6. **Two acts on each side, authority apart from performance** (operator, verbatim: *"A"*, 2026-11-03). **Issue** by the coordinator as Authorizing Officer, then **hang** by the repair dispatch citing the issue; **clearance authorized** by the coordinator, then **clear** by the restoration dispatch, recording the position found (TUM 1.3.2(3), 1.8.1, 1.8.5.b). Each is one `gz` verb appending one event (ruling 4). Refusal 1 compares the hanging and clearing dispatch ids; refusals 2 and 6 read the hung and cleared state. Until RP-13 lands, a hang records the isolation and the hangar's blanket still lowers the guards; once RP-13 retires the blanket, the hang event is what lowers that control at its place of call. Put to the operator: A, this; B, one act each side, the coordinator recording the hang, against A3; C, A with the hang enforcing the isolation now, pulling RP-13's retirement of the blanket into RP-12 against route A.
7. **The not-available mark is derived and stamped** (operator, verbatim: *"A"*, 2026-11-03). The clearance-authorized verb reads the hanging dispatch's state from the ledger: ended, and the event records the repair activity *not available* (TUM 1.8.4.a); still live, and its concurrence is required (TUM 1.8.1, *"when applicable"*). Every clearance says whose concurrence was absent, and no one marks it by hand. Put to the operator: A, this; B, implicit, the field dropped, which loses the record and lets a live repair dispatch be cleared around; C, the coordinator marking it on every clearance, a field of byte-identical text with no channel (GHI #764). With rulings 3 to 7, open point 3 is closed.
8. **The maintenance record is ledger events, rendered as a view** (operator, verbatim: *"A"*, 2026-11-03). Each act appends one entry event carrying what was done, its commits, the measurement before and after, the performer's dispatch id (ruling 1), the order id and the MX session; the order's Gate 5 event is the approver's signature that closes it. Each element of 14 CFR § 43.9(a) has its field: description, completion date (the event's timestamp), performer (the dispatch id), approver (Gate 5). The passage log becomes a view over these events and is no longer hand-kept. Put to the operator: A, this; B, git history with the `Task:` trailer as the entry, which leaves the measurement and the performer nowhere under the closed trailer set; C, a hand-editable file per order.
9. **The list of discrepancies is ledger events, rendered as a view; a line that leaves the package becomes a problem report** (operator, verbatim: *"A"*, 2026-11-03). A discrepancy is recorded with its grade (*blocks return to service* or *due by a named event*), its order and MX session, and its source (left broken, an inspector's rejection, or found in the work); a later event clears it with its evidence; root cause and corrective action are carried only when the line blocks return to service or recurs (`data/recurrence_threshold.json`, `gz insights recurrence`). A line that leaves the package's scope, above the check level or carried past the package's close, is filed as a GitHub issue through `ghi-author`, and the discrepancy event cites it: the issue stays the problem report (B12), and return to service reads the list without the network. Put to the operator: A, this; B, every discrepancy a GitHub issue, which merges a discrepancy into a problem report and needs the network at return to service; C, A with each blocking line mirrored as an issue, one fact in two homes.
10. **Open lines carried, the rest frozen as written** (operator, verbatim: *"A"*, 2026-11-03). Once the three stores land, each `open` line of the contract's § List of discrepancies (45 at 2026-11-03, a dated count) is recorded as one event in the record the contract's own map names (a temporary isolation a tag-out line item; a *retired* or *replaced* line, or the new form of a *reconnected* one, a maintenance record entry; outstanding work a discrepancy), citing the line it came from. Cleared lines and the whole § Passage log stay as written, a dated record, as the `RP-n` ruling kept dated records. From that act both sections take no new lines and point to the views. The carry is one act under RP-12, its line-by-line mapping reviewed by the operator, and the contract's § Records changes by its own amendment at that act (B1). Put to the operator: A, this; B, everything carried, the passage log back-filled as maintenance record events, whose dispatch ids and measures were never recorded in that form; C, everything frozen and the open lines hand-kept until each closes, two systems in parallel that return to service and refusal 6 would not both read. With rulings 8 to 10, open point 4 is closed.
11. **A stop holds what its trigger reaches** (operator, verbatim: *"a"*, 2026-11-04). The closed trigger list and its routing stand as the record's C4 ruled them (operator: *"A: routed by trigger"*). An incorrect, incomplete or unclear procedure (JFMM I-1.6.11.a), rework inside scope (I-2.3.7.2), a result outside its specification, a deletion, history-rewrite or force-push stop, and an inspector's deficiency (I-1.6.15.k, C6) each hold the **order** they arose on. A tag discrepancy (TUM 1.8.5.b) and a surveillance hold (A8) also hold the **surface**, across every order that touches it, since the hazard belongs to the surface. A change of scope, boundary or test (I-2.3.7.3) holds the **package**, which holds the scope. Refusal 4 widens to landing on a held order, surface or package, which the witness floor allows. This brings C4's package-level stop onto ruling 2's order and ruling 5's typed surface. Put to the operator: A, this; B, every stop holds the whole package, as C4 was written, so one unclear procedure halts every unrelated order; C, every stop holds only its order, which lets a sibling order keep working on a surface under a surveillance hold or a tag discrepancy.
12. **A stop is the shared block, extended** (operator, verbatim: *"A"*, 2026-11-04). A stop is a `gz wp block` event that also carries the trigger from the closed list, its reach (order, surface or package, ruling 11), the named resolver, and the stop request it answers where there is one. A resolution is a `gz wp unblock` event, which the runtime refuses from anyone but the named resolver. Today's block stands as the case whose resolver is the operator. The fence and refusal 4 read one holding record for both pipelines, as RO-14's ruling 4 shares block and unblock. Put to the operator: A, this; B, separate stop and hold events beside an untouched block, two holding records with nothing keeping them in step; C, block and unblock retired for stop events in both pipelines, which reaches into construction's runtime, RP-14's to migrate.
13. **A resolution is a typed answer fitted to its trigger, with evidence the ledger resolves** (operator, verbatim: *"A"*, 2026-11-04). Every resolution carries the resolver's mark, the coordinator's dispatch id (ruling 1) or the operator's words verbatim, and one answer from a closed list; the runtime refuses an answer its trigger does not allow. An unclear procedure takes a pen-and-ink change citing the commit that amends the procedure, or **escalate**, which clears nothing, hands the stop to the operator and cites the prior answer. Rework inside scope takes rework done, citing its maintenance record entries (ruling 8). A tag discrepancy takes tag corrected, citing the tag-out events (rulings 4 to 6). A change of scope takes a change order, the operator's words with the package's scope amended. A result outside its specification takes a change order or an accepted departure from specification, which the handback's transfer of custody lists. A deletion, history-rewrite or force-push stop takes the operator's ruling. A surveillance hold takes the coordinator's resolution citing evidence events, or the operator's lift. An inspector's deficiency takes the buy-back, open point 6's. A resolution with no evidence, or evidence the ledger does not resolve, is refused. Put to the operator: A, this; B, free-text resolution and the resolver's identity, which lets a departure from specification clear without reaching the handback; C, A with the runtime re-checking the evidence per trigger, addable later since A records it. With rulings 11 to 13, open point 5 is closed.
14. **A fixed floor of (I) points, final inspection at the order** (operator, verbatim: *"A"*, 2026-11-04). The QAI (A5), the three tiers (C3), rework and buy-back (C5), and final inspection and acceptance (C10) stand as the record ruled them. Every repair order carries an (I) point at each tag hanging, before commencement; at each clearing; at each restored control's red witness; and at the order's **final inspection**, which checks the order's record complete, every tag it hung cleared, and its surfaces restored to normal. The operator's Gate 5 on the order follows its final inspection. Return to service at the package's close reads every order's final inspection and Gate 5, and is no second inspection. No (I) point falls inside the work. This brings C10's package-level final inspection onto ruling 2's order. Put to the operator: A, this; B, final inspection kept at the package as C10 was written, so each order's Gate 5 attests a record no one independent has checked; C, A plus points the coordinator designates at the freeze, which lets the one directing the work choose what is inspected, against A5 and refusal 3; points the operator designates can be added later without changing A.
15. **An (I) mark is the shared review record, extended** (operator, verbatim: *"A"*, 2026-11-04). Each review in the acceptance store carries its tier, second person or inspection, and an (I) review names the point it signs: a tag's hanging or clearing, a control's red witness, or the final inspection (ruling 14). The tier is read from the reviewer's dispatch record (ruling 1), never supplied by the caller, so only a QAI dispatch signs an (I) mark; this meets refusal 3 and C3's refusal of self-signing. The readiness reducer requires an (I) review at every fixed point before an order reads ready. Construction's reviews are unchanged and record their tier as second person. Acceptance stays the one shared runtime of RO-14's ruling 4. Put to the operator: A, this; B, a separate inspection verb and events outside the acceptance store, two stores readiness must join; C, an ordinary review tagged `qai` by the caller, the value anyone can supply that leaves refusal 3 unmet.
